# HUMAN.md

## A note from the creator

With the emergence of agentic coding tools, development feels much faster to me. I see real value in being able to explore an idea and turn it into something working more easily. But I also worry that the emphasis is drifting toward producing more, with too little attention to whether the work is useful, considered, or worth maintaining. I want us to use these tools to do worthwhile things well.

That concern is where HUMAN.md began. In a world that seems to move faster all the time, especially in technology, AI, and development, I want to make a little room to slow down and collect our thoughts. For me, HUMAN.md is also a small, symbolic invitation: take a pen or an iPad, make a good cup of coffee or tea, and give yourself time to think about what you want to achieve with the project.

Before writing code or asking an agent to generate it, I want to pause and ask: why should this project exist? What am I trying to achieve? What questions am I asking? What do I expect to learn? I don't need all the answers before starting. An experiment can be a good first step if I can explain what I hope to learn from it.

I can ask an agent to help explore an idea, question my assumptions, and carry out the work. I still need to choose the direction, review the results, and take responsibility for the code I use and how I use it. Having something that runs doesn't settle whether it serves the purpose I had in mind.

As we discussed this idea, I realized that focusing only on the beginning was too narrow. My questions and assumptions can change while building, and an unexpected result can change what I think the project should become. I want to leave enough of that reasoning visible that someone else, or I myself later, can understand how the direction developed.

I like to think of HUMAN.md as a fireplace for that process: a place where you and your team can gather your thoughts, talk through what matters, and return when the direction feels unclear. It doesn't have to begin in a Markdown file. The thinking might happen on paper, on a tablet, or in a conversation; HUMAN.md can give what you want to carry forward a home alongside the project.

That is the commitment I want HUMAN.md to represent. I don't have a perfect structure for these thoughts, and I don't think every project needs the same one. A note like this, open questions, reflections on findings, or links to research and decisions can all be useful. The structure should follow the project. I don't want filling in headings to take the place of thinking.

I don't know yet whether a small Markdown file will help with this. I want to try it, listen to people who question the idea, and learn from using it. This is my thinking so far, and I expect it to change.
